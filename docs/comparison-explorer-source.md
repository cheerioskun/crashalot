# Flight-type and aircraft comparison prototype

November 6, 2026. These chapters replace all three former placeholder slides. The narrative explores whether perceptions transfer across different operations, then asks whether aircraft/model/age differences matter. The archive alone does not establish a per-flight risk ordering, a manufacturer safety ranking, or a measured effect on public perception.

## Retained source and scope

`public/data/comparison-records.json` projects the existing PlaneCrashData snapshot, revision and SHA-256 from `data/sources.json`. `scripts/build_comparison_records.py` reads that retained file only, checks its checksum, preserves original string IDs, and supports `--check`. No new data download or production-count-to-flight-exposure substitution is used.

Scope: worldwide, 1970–2024, known airborne phase (departure, in flight, arrival), using the same phase mapping as the Sankey. There are 12,691 distinct aircraft-occurrence records across operations; 393 ground/unresolved-phase records are excluded. Scheduled Revenue Flight contributes the same 2,792 IDs as the preceding Sankey. The seven named flight types are shown as annual frequency trails. The 2,856 heterogeneous remaining missions and missing types stay in the JSON and the default All kinds of flying cohort, but are omitted as a combined trail; only 14 of these have no source flight type. They are not merged into scheduled revenue.

Aircraft age is calendar year of crash minus recorded manufacture year. Values missing or outside `1903 <= manufacture year <= crash year` are unusable. The user requested that these be skipped in the scatterplot and its linked breakdown: 398 records across the full comparison cohort. Flight-type counts retain them. Calendar-year age is approximate, not exact aircraft service time.

Manufacturer labels are explicit literal prefixes of the aircraft-name string, matched longest-first. They are not a manufacturer source field or a corporate ownership history. Recognized source names remain separate (for example, Douglas is not reassigned to Boeing); 176 unmatched records remain Unclassified name after the November 6 audit recognized 401 additional records through verified literal prefixes, including the no-space ATR42/ATR72 boundary. Unclassified names are omitted from manufacturer choices and bars; they remain in the all-manufacturer scatter and model packing. Raw aircraft strings and manufacture years remain inspectable. The aircraft-production dataset has no direct manufacturer join and its production counts are not flight exposure, so it is not joined into a risk calculation.

## Selection contract

All kinds of flying is selected by default. A shared year interval (inclusive start, exclusive end) and any explicit flight-type choice carry between the two chapters. The aircraft scatter plots one mark per usable-age source record at crash date × age. Horizontal D3 brushing snaps to calendar years; numeric year controls supply a keyboard alternative. Unknown ages are excluded from both the scatter and its linked selection views.

All aircraft-linked manufacturer/model cells are derived from selected original IDs, never from display labels or point array indices. A manufacturer choice narrows the highlighted scatter records and model packing. Choosing a model selects its schematic without narrowing the crash-record cohort. Manufacturer bars retain the date/type context for comparison. The flight-type chapter retains age-unknown records and states that broader scope in source details; a shared time window is not a claim that its age-independent count equals the aircraft-only subset.

## Fatal-outcome emphasis

The flight-type chapter now uses the frequency trails described below. Each manufacturer bar uses a shared zero baseline for the solid fatal segment (`fatalities > 0`), followed by the pale nonfatal remainder. The two segments sum to the original total; the archive's source IDs are retained in both total and fatal membership. Direct labels emphasize fatal counts, with hover and keyboard-focus tooltips disclosing fatal, nonfatal and total counts. These are counts, not risk rates.

## Packed aircraft models and schematics

The model bars and selected-record list are replaced by a dense, single-level D3 circle packing. All model labels in the current known-age selection participate (479 labels across 12,293 records in the full view). This has an Apollonian-like appearance; it is a weighted packing, not a mathematical Apollonian gasket with prescribed radii. Circle **area**, rather than radius, is proportional to source record count. No decorative filler circles or top-N truncation are used. Circles partition the selected original IDs exactly.

Yellow hue (HSL 46°) and lightness (62%) are fixed; HSL saturation equals `fatalCount / count`. The denominator is all recorded crashes for that model in the current selection, not all flights or aircraft in service. Hover/focus exposes the model, total, fatal count and percentage. These shares do not estimate model failure probabilities.

Click or Enter/Space selects the schematic at right. Arrow keys move within the circles; a native model chooser supplies an alternative for tiny circles. The selected model persists when it remains in the filtered population; otherwise the largest remaining model is selected. An empty selection clears the old aircraft. Model selection never changes the time/manufacturer/type cohort.

The Polaroid reuses the opening slide’s ivory frame, source credit and tilted entrance: 650 ms opacity fade and 850 ms translated/rotated rise. Reduced motion presents it immediately. Public schematic previews cover 23 of the fixed top 30 model labels by full-selection count. The extended catalogue also covers all 11 Airbus labels and 30 of the 31 Boeing labels in this view, including manufacturer PDF figures for A300-600, A350-900 and 737 MAX 8. KC-137 and other unsourced models retain explicit placeholders. Family-level drawings are identified in captions. See [aircraft-schematic-coverage.md](aircraft-schematic-coverage.md) for unmatched or paid-only variants, manufacturer PDF figures and all source pages. The original retained dataset remains downloadable in Source, scope & selection.

## Brush-edge age distribution and cohort guide

The horizontal selection carries a right-edge age histogram facing outward. A reserved plot gutter keeps the full-range distribution inside the SVG. Five-year bins cover the full valid-age domain, sharing the scatterplot's vertical age scale. Bin memberships partition the exact selected source IDs, including the manufacturer filter; no record is counted twice. Each bin uses the same fatal/nonfatal segmentation as the comparison bars. Width is normalized to the largest bin in the current selection, labeled above the histogram; it is not a probability or an exposure-adjusted age-risk curve. The right baseline follows the inclusive calendar selection's exclusive end boundary. Full-range selection pins the histogram to the plot’s right edge. During dragging its baseline follows the live brush edge, and updates from fields or filters ease over 180 ms. Clicking outside the selection clears the year brush. Fatal segments use the historical chapter’s yellow/gold palette in both themes.

The annotation describes increasing spread, not uniform randomness. It compares the middle 80% (10th–90th percentiles) of crash-record ages in 1970–1989 and 2005–2024, recomputed for the active operation/manufacturer context. Two brackets mark those ranges. If either context is sparse or the later span does not increase, the annotation uses a neutral range statement. A brush changes linked distributions and source IDs, while this full-timeline annotation continues to describe the displayed context.

The source builder’s `--check --age-audit` mode reproduces the comparison. Across all operations the central-80% span widens from 28 to 39 years (5,833 and 2,960 known-age records); scheduled services widen from 24.9 to 32 years (1,502 and 476 records). Later five-year age bins remain unequal, so neither uniformity nor random crash occurrence is asserted. These distributions condition on crashes and do not estimate risk by aircraft age.

## Guided reveals and maintenance context

Flight types retain four cues. Aircraft have five cues: **0 · Due for an oil change**; **1 · the mandated-inspection explanation and scaffold fade in together over 650 ms**; **2 · records pour in chronologically over 1.8 seconds**; **3 · the populated chart holds**; **3.5 · the age-distribution annotation fades in**. Beat 3 holds the complete data for 650 ms before the annotation. The explanation remains visible throughout. Auto stays inside the chapter and stops at the annotation. Next settles unfinished motion before advancing. Back rewinds, Replay restarts, Pause/Resume and document/slide visibility suspend reveals. Reduced-motion mode shows a static final view. Once the graph appears, a deliberate data interaction opens the final exploration state immediately and holds Auto. The visible scope line omits “Recorded airborne phases”; the actual source cohort is unchanged and documented here and in source details.

The maintenance explanation is explicitly U.S. context: FAA AC 120-84, Aging Aircraft Inspections and Records Reviews (active guidance, change 1), and 49 U.S.C. § 44717, accessed November 6, 2026. These describe inspections, records reviews and adequate, timely maintenance of age-sensitive parts for covered air-carrier operations. They do not establish worldwide compliance or quantify the contribution of age to crash probability. The archive contains crash records only; fleet age and flight exposure by age are missing, so the story does not claim that age is negligible as a risk factor.

## Annual flight-type frequency trails

The bars in chapter 6 are replaced by seven trails for 1970–2024 (or the shared brush period when revisiting from the aircraft view). X is calendar year; height above each row’s baseline is the annual recorded crash count. Every row uses the same vertical count scale, set by the largest annual total in the displayed period. Monotone curves interpolate the reviewed annual observations; they are not kernel-density estimates. The pale total area is overlaid by the yellow fatal subset. Heights are never normalized separately by type.

Each annual cell retains all original source IDs and its fatal subset. Zero-count years remain inspectable. Hover and focus expose exact year and period totals; Left/Right and Home/End inspect years. Click or Enter/Space chooses the operation for the aircraft chapter; choosing the active trail again restores all kinds. The old year/type filter row and selected-count paragraph are removed from this chapter. Aircraft filters remain on the aircraft chapter. An aircraft manufacturer choice does not silently filter the flight-type trails; the shared date window still carries between chapters.

Four beats remain: introductory headline and scaffold; scheduled-service trail grows; remaining trails grow; final narration and exploration. Whole-trail amplitude animation uses the same real beat clock as the shared footer timer, and Pause/Back/reduced motion retain their existing behavior. Auto waits for Next after the last beat.
